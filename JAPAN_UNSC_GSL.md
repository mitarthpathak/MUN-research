# JAPAN — General Speakers' List (GSL)

**Committee:** UN Security Council (College MUN)
**Agenda:** Addressing Middle East Crises and Strengthening Maritime Security Amid the Militarization of Strategic Waterways
**Facts checked as of:** 1 November 2026

---

## 1. MAIN GSL (about 90 seconds)

> Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates,
>
> The Strait of Hormuz, the artery for a fifth of the world's oil, is all but closed. Ships are still being struck, and thousands of innocent seafarers remain trapped in the Gulf.
>
> For Japan, this is not distant news. Nine of every ten barrels of our crude oil pass through Hormuz. In the Red Sea, the Japanese-operated *Galaxy Leader* was seized and its crew held for fourteen months, which is why Japan co-sponsored Resolution 2722. But when a strait closes, Japan does not pay alone. Every family, from Asia to Africa to Europe, pays.
>
> Japan's position rests on law. Under UNCLOS, transit passage through international straits cannot be suspended, and it cannot be put up for sale. Attacks on civilian vessels must stop, whoever carries them out. And there is no maritime security without regional peace. Japan calls for full implementation of Resolution 2803 in Gaza, unhindered aid, and a two-state solution.
>
> Japan proposes three steps. First, activate the IMO safe corridor Japan co-proposed, and bring stranded seafarers home. Second, an IMO-facilitated information-sharing mechanism with 24/7 hotlines, so that one incident at sea does not become a war. Third, any naval protection must be defensive, lawful, and accountable to this Council.
>
> Japan seeks security through law, commerce through open seas, and peace through diplomacy. We invite every delegation to build it with us. Thank you.

### Delivery notes
- **Pause** after "all but closed." Let the hook land.
- **Stress** these: *nine of every ten barrels*, *fourteen months*, *Japan does not pay alone*, *cannot be put up for sale*, *whoever carries them out*.
- Count the three proposals on your fingers: **First... Second... Third...**
- Slow down for the last line. It is the line people will remember.
- At a steady pace this runs about 1:30. If the Chair gives **60 seconds**, use version 2 below.

## 2. SHORT GSL (60 seconds)

> Honourable Chair, distinguished delegates,
>
> The Strait of Hormuz, the artery for a fifth of the world's oil, is all but closed, and thousands of seafarers remain trapped in the Gulf. For Japan, which receives nine of every ten barrels of its crude through Hormuz, and whose *Galaxy Leader* crew was held for fourteen months in the Red Sea, this is not distant news.
>
> Japan's position is simple. Under UNCLOS, transit passage cannot be suspended, or sold. Attacks on civilian ships must stop, by anyone. And there is no maritime security without regional peace, so Japan calls for full implementation of Resolution 2803 in Gaza and unhindered aid.
>
> Japan proposes to activate the IMO safe corridor Japan co-proposed, to create an IMO-led information-sharing mechanism with 24/7 hotlines, and to ensure any naval protection is defensive, lawful and accountable to this Council.
>
> Security through law, commerce through open seas, peace through diplomacy. Thank you.

## 4. EXPECTED POIs AND QUICK ANSWERS (20–30 sec each)

These use the prep-PPT formula: **POSITION → REASON → RESPONSE OR COMPROMISE.**

**Q: "Does Japan recognise the State of Palestine?"** (trap question, so be accurate)
> "Japan firmly supports a two-state solution. In 2025 the Japanese government said the question is not *whether* to recognise Palestine but *when*. Today Japan's focus is what saves lives: aid, reconstruction and a credible political horizon under Resolution 2803."

**Q (from Iran): "Iran never ratified UNCLOS, so transit passage does not bind us."**
> "The right of passage through international straits is older than UNCLOS. The International Court of Justice recognised it in the *Corfu Channel* case in 1949, and this Council reaffirmed navigational rights in the Strait in Resolution 2817. It is customary law, and it binds everyone."

**Q: "Will Japan send warships?"**
> "Japan acts within its Constitution and its laws. In 1991, after the Gulf War ceasefire, Japanese minesweepers helped clear the Gulf. Japan's government is studying options in that spirit, but a ceasefire must come first. Our immediate contribution is the corridor, information-sharing, and diplomacy."

**Q: "Is Japan just following the United States?"**
> "No. Japan's line, free passage with no additional costs, applies to everyone. Japan also speaks to Tehran directly: our Prime Minister and Foreign Minister have both raised Hormuz with Iran's leadership. Dialogue is not endorsement. It is how we prevent miscalculation."

**Q: "Isn't Japan only here for its oil?"**
> "Ninety-four percent of Japan's crude comes from the Middle East, so yes, our exposure is real. That makes us a responsible stakeholder, not a selfish one. With oil above a hundred dollars a barrel, every importing country pays, and the poorest pay most. Open straits are a global public good."

**Q: "Why do you condemn attacks on ships but not Israel?"**
> "Japan applies one standard to every party: international humanitarian law, protection of civilians and aid workers, and unhindered humanitarian access. That is why Japan voted for ceasefire texts in this Council and calls for full implementation of Resolution 2803."

**Q (motion from prep PPT, slide 10): "Does Japan support an international naval coalition?"**
> "Japan supports it **conditionally**. Protection of shipping, yes. But it needs a defensive mandate, full UNCLOS compliance, coordination with coastal states and the IMO, deconfliction hotlines, and regular reporting to this Council. Without safeguards, more warships in a narrow strait means more risk of miscalculation."
