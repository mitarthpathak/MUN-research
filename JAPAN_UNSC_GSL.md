# JAPAN — GSL and Moderated Caucus Speeches

**Committee:** UN Security Council (College MUN)
**Agenda:** Addressing Middle East Crises and Strengthening Maritime Security Amid the Militarization of Strategic Waterways
**Facts checked as of:** 1 November 2026

---

## 1. GSL (65 seconds)

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> Today, the Strait of Hormuz, which carries one-fifth of the world's oil, is almost closed. Thousands of sailors are stuck.
>
> For Japan, this is personal. Nine out of ten barrels of our oil pass through Hormuz. In the Red Sea, a Japanese-run ship was hijacked and its crew held for fourteen months. But when a sea route closes, the whole world pays.
>
> Under the Law of the Sea, no country can close an international strait or charge a fee to cross it. Attacks on ships must stop, no matter who does them. No peace on land means no safety at sea, so Japan backs the Gaza ceasefire and more aid.
>
> Japan proposes three steps. One, the safe sea corridor Japan proposed at the IMO, to bring sailors home. Two, 24/7 hotlines, so one mistake does not become a war. Three, navies protecting ships must stay defensive and report to this Council.
>
> Security through law. Trade through open seas. Peace through dialogue. Thank you.

**Length:** about 167 words, roughly 65 seconds at a steady pace. If your timer shows more than 65 seconds, drop the line "For Japan, this is personal."

### Memory trick: 5 blocks
**HORMUZ → PERSONAL → LAW → 1-2-3 → MOTTO**

| Block | What you're saying | Key words to remember |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | Hormuz is almost closed | *one-fifth of the world's oil, sailors stuck* |
| 2. Personal | Why Japan cares | *nine out of ten barrels, ship hijacked, fourteen months, whole world pays* |
| 3. Law | Japan's position | *can't close, can't charge a fee, no matter who, no peace on land* |
| 4. 1-2-3 | Japan's proposals | *corridor, hotlines, defensive navies* |
| 5. Motto | Close | *law, open seas, dialogue* |

### Delivery tips
- **Pause** after "almost closed."
- **Stress** these: *personal*, *nine out of ten*, *fourteen months*, *whole world pays*, *no matter who*.
- Count **One, Two, Three** on your fingers.
- Say the motto slowly. That's the line the chair will remember.

### If the news changes before committee
Qatar was mediating on Hormuz in the last week of October 2026, so **check the news on the morning of committee.** If a deal or ceasefire is announced, replace the first two lines with this:

> "Japan welcomes the steps to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. But an open strait is not yet a safe strait. Mines are still there, and sailors are still stuck."

### MOD 1 — Reopening the Strait of Hormuz

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> The Strait of Hormuz does not belong to one country. It belongs to world trade. Under the Law of the Sea, no country is allowed to shut an international strait. And passage must stay free: no tolls, no fees, no conditions. If one country charges a fee today, another will do it tomorrow, and every sea route in the world becomes a checkpoint.
>
> Japan asks for three things. First, stop all attacks on ships. Second, clear the mines safely. Third, talk about the future of Hormuz with everyone who uses it: the coastal states, the user states, and the IMO.
>
> The countries that depend on this strait deserve a seat at the table. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"Every sea route becomes a checkpoint."*

---

### MOD 2 — Red Sea and the Houthis

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> Japan knows this threat first-hand. In November 2023, the Houthis hijacked the *Galaxy Leader*, a ship run by a Japanese company. Its crew, sailors from five countries, was held for fourteen months. They had nothing to do with any war.
>
> That is why Japan co-sponsored Resolution 2722, which demands that these attacks stop. Yet this year, the attacks have started again. Ships are forced to go around Africa, adding up to two weeks and about one million dollars in extra fuel per trip.
>
> No political cause justifies attacking merchant ships or kidnapping sailors. Japan asks this Council to enforce Resolution 2722 and stop weapons from reaching the attackers. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"No political cause justifies attacking merchant ships."*

---

### MOD 3 — Naval Coalitions and Militarization

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> Japan's answer to a naval coalition is simple: yes, but with rules.
>
> Ships need protection. But more warships in a narrow strait also means more chances of a mistake. One wrong signal, and a small incident becomes a regional war.
>
> So Japan supports protection with four rules. One, defensive only: escort ships, don't start fights. Two, follow international law. Three, work with coastal states and the IMO. Four, report regularly to this Council. In 2020, Japan sent its own information-gathering mission to the region, built for watching and sharing, not fighting.
>
> And every navy in the area needs a hotline to every other navy. Protection without communication is just escalation. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"Protection without communication is just escalation."*

---

### MOD 4 — Protecting Stranded Sailors

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> Behind every ship is a crew. Today, thousands of sailors are stuck in the Gulf. They are far from home, unable to leave, and in danger from missiles and mines. Many come from countries that have nothing to do with this war.
>
> In March, the IMO backed a plan for a safe sea corridor to get these ships and sailors out. Japan helped propose that plan. But a plan on paper saves no one.
>
> Japan asks this Council to make the corridor work now, with every side promising not to attack ships using it. Give crews medical help and contact with their families. Release every detained sailor. Sailors are civilians, not bargaining chips. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"Sailors are civilians, not bargaining chips."*

---

### MOD 5 — Gaza: Aid and Resolution 2803

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> The Gaza ceasefire is now one year old. That matters. The living hostages are home, and the large-scale fighting has stopped. But a ceasefire is not peace. Aid is still far below what people need, rebuilding has barely started, and civilians are still being killed.
>
> Japan welcomed Resolution 2803 and promised help with aid, rebuilding and governance. Now it must be fully carried out: more aid trucks every day, safety for aid workers, and real steps toward a two-state solution, where Israelis and Palestinians both live in peace and security.
>
> Japan condemns terrorism, and Japan protects civilians. These are not opposites. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"A ceasefire is not peace."*

---

### MOD 6 — De-escalation: Iran, Israel and the US

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> This war has already spread from land to sea: to Gulf cities, to Hormuz, and to the Red Sea. Every new strike makes the next one more likely. Japan's message to all sides is the same: stop, and talk.
>
> Japan is a close ally of the United States. Japan also talks directly to Iran. Our Prime Minister and Foreign Minister have both raised Hormuz with Tehran. Talking is not taking sides. It is how you stop mistakes.
>
> Japan supports the mediation by Qatar and Oman, and asks Iran to stop attacking ships and its neighbours. We propose a regional contact group under the UN Secretary-General, so that coastal states and user states meet before the next crisis, not after it. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"Talking is not taking sides."*

---

### MOD 7 — Economic Impact and Energy Security

> Honourable Chair, delegates,
>
> This crisis is not only fought with missiles. It is felt at every petrol pump and grocery store in the world. Oil has gone above 100 dollars. Insurance for ships has shot up. Ships going around Africa burn more fuel and arrive late. The poorest countries, which never chose this war, pay the most.
>
> Japan is preparing. We are rebuilding our oil reserves and backing pipelines that avoid Hormuz. But no country can fully escape a closed strait.
>
> So Japan proposes that the IMO, insurers and shipping nations work together. Keep insurance affordable, share risk warnings in real time, and move food and medicine first. A safe sea is the cheapest policy the world can buy. Thank you.

**Closing line to remember:** *"A safe sea is the cheapest policy the world can buy."*

### Surprise mod topic? Use this formula

1. **Problem (1 line):** "This issue is not only regional. It affects every country that trades by sea."
2. **Japan's link (1 fact):** use one of *nine out of ten barrels*, the *Galaxy Leader*, Resolution 2722, or the IMO corridor.
3. **Japan's ask (1–2 points):** pick from *stop attacks on ships*, *hotlines*, *IMO-led cooperation*, *more aid*, *talks with all sides*.
4. **Close:** one of the closing lines above, or the motto.

## 3. EXPECTED POIs AND QUICK ANSWERS (20–30 sec each)

These use the prep-PPT formula: **POSITION → REASON → RESPONSE OR COMPROMISE.**

**Q: "Does Japan recognise the State of Palestine?"** (trap question, so answer carefully)
> "Japan strongly supports a two-state solution. In 2025, Japan said the question is not *whether* to recognise Palestine, but *when*. Right now, Japan is focused on what saves lives: aid, rebuilding, and a real path to peace under Resolution 2803."

**Q (from Iran): "Iran never ratified UNCLOS, so it does not bind us."**
> "The right to pass through international straits is older than UNCLOS. The International Court of Justice recognised it in the *Corfu Channel* case in 1949, and this Council reaffirmed navigation rights in the Strait in Resolution 2817. It binds everyone."

**Q: "Will Japan send warships?"**
> "Japan acts within its Constitution and its laws. In 1991, after the Gulf War ceasefire, Japanese minesweepers helped clear the Gulf. Japan is studying options in that spirit, but a ceasefire must come first. Right now, Japan contributes through the corridor, information-sharing, and diplomacy."

**Q: "Is Japan just following the United States?"**
> "No. Japan's rule, free passage with no extra fees, applies to everyone. Japan also talks to Tehran directly. Talking is not taking sides. It is how we stop mistakes."

**Q: "Isn't Japan only here for its oil?"**
> "Ninety-four percent of Japan's crude comes from the Middle East, so yes, our risk is real. That makes us a responsible stakeholder, not a selfish one. With oil above 100 dollars, every country pays, and the poorest pay the most."

**Q: "Why do you condemn attacks on ships but not Israel?"**
> "Japan uses one standard for everyone: protect civilians and aid workers, follow humanitarian law, and let aid in. That is why Japan voted for ceasefire texts in this Council and calls for full implementation of Resolution 2803."

**Q (prep-PPT motion): "Does Japan support an international naval coalition?"**
> "Yes, but with rules. It must be defensive, follow the Law of the Sea, work with coastal states and the IMO, and report to this Council. Protection without communication is just escalation."
